# Persona Design Principles

Core principles for evidence-based, behavior-centered persona design. Reference this file when explaining why personas are constructed a particular way or when a user asks about the theoretical grounding.

## Nine Principles

**1. Evidence-based.** Personas should be grounded in observed data — interviews, usage logs, support tickets — not imagination. Where evidence is absent, label assumptions explicitly and assign Exploratory confidence.

**2. Goal-oriented.** Personas represent what users are trying to accomplish, not who they are demographically. Primary and secondary goals, success criteria, and motivations matter more than age or job title. *Design should optimize for user goals, not user traits.*

**3. Behavior-centered.** Focus on observable behaviors: workflows, decision strategies, coping mechanisms, workarounds. What users *do* under constraint matters more than what they say they prefer.

**4. Representative, not exhaustive.** Each persona captures a dominant pattern across many users — it abstracts away irrelevant variation. Too many personas dilute focus; too few oversimplify. 3–5 personas is a reasonable target for most projects.

**5. Contextually grounded.** Personas are only meaningful within their context of use: physical, social, organizational, technical, regulatory. Without context, personas become generic and non-actionable.

**6. Actionable for design decisions.** A persona is only valuable if it changes decisions. If a persona cannot be used to justify or reject a design choice, it is incomplete. Ask: *"What would this persona need here?"*

**7. Internally coherent.** Each persona must present a consistent logic linking goals, behaviors, constraints, and motivations. Contradictions should be intentional (competing incentives) and explained, not accidental.

**8. Explicit about assumptions.** Personas simplify reality. High-quality personas state their data sources, identify uncertainties, and clarify which populations are not represented — preventing misuse and overgeneralization.

**9. Living hypotheses.** Personas are not static deliverables. They should evolve as new data arrives, systems change, or design questions shift. Treat them as hypotheses to refine, not truths to preserve.
